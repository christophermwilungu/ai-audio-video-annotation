Multimedia Annotation Quality Control

Purpose

Quality control helps ensure that audio and video annotations are accurate, consistent, complete, and suitable for potential AI training and evaluation workflows.

Audio Quality Checks

Review audio annotations for:

- Accurate start and end timestamps
- Correct speaker identification
- Accurate transcription
- Correct speech/non-speech classification
- Appropriate background-sound labels
- Audio clarity and quality
- Consistent formatting

Video Quality Checks

Review video annotations for:

- Accurate timestamps
- Correct object identification
- Correct activity/action labels
- Appropriate scene classification
- Accurate event descriptions
- Visibility and video quality
- Consistent formatting

Common Annotation Errors

Examples of errors that should be identified during review:

1. Incorrect timestamps
2. Missing annotations
3. Inconsistent labels
4. Incorrect speaker identification
5. Incorrect object or activity labels
6. Unsupported assumptions
7. Poor transcription
8. Incomplete records

Quality Review Process

1. Review the annotation against the source media.
2. Check timestamps.
3. Verify labels.
4. Check transcription or event descriptions.
5. Identify missing or inconsistent information.
6. Correct errors where appropriate.
7. Flag uncertain cases for further review.

Quality Principles

Annotations should be:

- Accurate
- Consistent
- Complete
- Clear
- Objective
- Reproducible

Annotation Status

Each reviewed record can be categorized as:

- Accepted — Meets quality requirements.
- Needs Review — Contains uncertainty or requires additional checking.
- Rejected — Does not meet the required annotation criteria.
