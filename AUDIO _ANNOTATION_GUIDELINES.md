Audio Annotation Guidelines

Purpose

These guidelines define a consistent approach for labeling and reviewing audio data for potential AI training and evaluation applications.

Annotation Categories

1. Speech

Label a segment as Speech when a person is clearly speaking.

2. Non-Speech

Use Non-Speech for periods without spoken language, including silence or non-verbal vocal sounds.

3. Speaker

Identify different speakers using consistent labels such as:

- Speaker 1
- Speaker 2
- Speaker 3

4. Background Sound

Identify important environmental or background sounds when they are relevant to the annotation task.

Examples include:

- Music
- Traffic
- Crowd noise
- Door closing
- Clapping
- Machinery

5. Timestamp

Record the beginning and ending time of each annotated segment.

Example:

"00:00.00 – 00:05.50"

Transcription

When transcription is required:

- Record the spoken words accurately.
- Preserve the meaning of the speaker's words.
- Avoid adding information that was not spoken.
- Mark unclear speech consistently.

Audio Quality

Review each segment for:

- Clarity
- Background noise
- Distortion
- Missing audio
- Overlapping speakers
- Volume problems

Annotation Principles

Annotators should:

1. Follow the task instructions consistently.
2. Use the same labels throughout the dataset.
3. Record accurate timestamps.
4. Avoid guessing unclear information.
5. Flag uncertain segments for quality review.
6. Maintain confidentiality when handling restricted data.

Quality Standard

An annotation should be considered acceptable when the label, timestamp, transcription, and relevant audio classification accurately represent the segment.
