Video Annotation Guidelines

Purpose

These guidelines provide a consistent approach for labeling video data for potential AI training, evaluation, and multimodal AI applications.

Annotation Categories

1. Object Identification

Identify relevant objects visible in each video segment.

Examples:

- Person
- Vehicle
- Phone
- Computer
- Book
- Laboratory equipment

2. Activity / Action

Label the main activity taking place in the segment.

Examples:

- Walking
- Sitting
- Speaking
- Writing
- Picking up an object
- Using equipment

3. Scene / Event

Describe the overall event or scene when required.

Examples:

- Classroom
- Laboratory
- Office
- Outdoor environment
- Meeting

4. Timestamp

Record the beginning and ending time of each relevant event.

Example:

"00:05.00 – 00:10.00"

5. Video Quality

Review each segment for:

- Visibility
- Lighting
- Camera stability
- Obstructions
- Motion blur
- Resolution
- Missing or corrupted frames

Annotation Principles

Annotators should:

1. Follow the task instructions consistently.
2. Use standardized labels.
3. Record accurate timestamps.
4. Describe observable actions rather than assumptions.
5. Avoid guessing information that cannot be confirmed visually.
6. Flag unclear or ambiguous segments for review.

Quality Standard

A video annotation is acceptable when the timestamp, object/activity label, scene description, and quality assessment accurately represent the visible content of the video segment.
